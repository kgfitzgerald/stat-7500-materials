Goals

The main goal of this assignment is to practice selecting, constructing, and interpreting visualizations that are appropriate for the variables and research questions being examined.

You will…

  • Investigate the source and collection of a real-world dataset
  • Inspect the structure and dimensions of a dataset
  • Visualize categorical and numerical variables
  • Compare counts and proportions in bar charts
  • Compare distributions using boxplots and ridge plots
  • Evaluate whether a visualization is appropriate for a particular question
  • Apply principles of effective data visualization
  • Interpret visualizations in context and identify limitations

The individual R commands matter, but the larger goal is to develop a reliable process: begin with a meaningful question, identify the relevant variables and their types, select an appropriate visualization, and interpret what the visualization does—and does not—show.

Data: 2020 American National Election Study

The data for this homework assignment come from the 2020 American National Election Study.

Import the data using the code below:

anes <- read_csv("data/anes2020_subset.csv")

The dataset contains the following variables:

  • CASEID: a unique identification number for each respondent
  • hunt_fish: whether the respondent went hunting or fishing in the past year
  • scientists: a feeling-thermometer rating indicating how warmly the respondent feels toward scientists, ranging from 0 (coolest) to 100 (warmest)
  • education: an ordinal categorical variable representing the respondent’s highest level of education, ranging from less than high school to a professional degree
  • ideology: a seven-point self-rating scale ranging from most liberal to most conservative
  • urbanrural: the type of community in which the respondent lives, with the possible values rural, small town, suburb, and city
